Applying it at Shopify
Prospect from evidence, timing, and account change.
Same operating system, different market The way I created this deal is how I would build pipeline at Shopify: start with account history, find a live signal, get ground-level truth, then move up with a business case.

How I would prospect at Shopify

My goal would be to avoid generic enterprise outbound. I would build account hypotheses around moments where commerce complexity, growth, or platform cost has changed enough to create a reason to revisit the stack.

1. Closed-lost first

I would start with the accounts Shopify has already learned something about. I want to know why we lost, who cared, what blocked the deal, which platform they chose, what commercial or technical assumption killed momentum, and what has changed since.

2. Then find the change

A closed-lost record becomes valuable when a new trigger appears. The outreach should connect the old blocker to a current signal, not simply ask whether they are ready to re-evaluate.

Platform signal
Replatforming or renewal
  • Salesforce Commerce Cloud, Adobe Commerce, custom stack, or legacy platform renewal
  • Replatforming RFP or agency-led redesign
  • New headless or composable initiative
Cost signal
Complexity is getting expensive
  • Large development backlog
  • Heavy middleware or agency dependency
  • Rising maintenance and total cost of ownership
Growth signal
The business is expanding
  • New countries or currencies
  • New brands or acquisitions
  • Large DTC push or international launch
Channel signal
B2B, retail, or omnichannel change
  • Wholesale growth or manual B2B workflows
  • Retail store expansion or POS modernization
  • Fragmented online and in-store customer experience
Leadership signal
Someone new owns the problem
  • New CIO, CTO, CDO, or VP Ecommerce
  • New commerce or digital transformation mandate
  • Hiring around ecommerce architecture or platform migration
Experience signal
Revenue is being constrained
  • Checkout friction or conversion pressure
  • Slow release cycles during peak periods
  • Localization or market launch limitations
My outbound formula: closed-lost context + fresh signal + ground-level evidence + a specific business hypothesis.

Where I would get ground-level intelligence

Before asking an executive to reconsider a platform decision, I want to understand how the current stack behaves for the teams living in it every day.

Digital commerceEcommerce product / site teams

Release speed, merchandising limitations, checkout friction, experimentation, conversion.

TechnologyArchitects / engineering leaders

Platform complexity, integrations, developer effort, reliability, maintenance burden.

RetailStore operations / POS leaders

Online and in-store fragmentation, inventory visibility, associate workflows.

B2BWholesale / B2B operators

Manual ordering, pricing complexity, customer portals, sales rep workflows.

GrowthInternational / market owners

Localization, currencies, catalogs, market launches, regional complexity.

RevenueCRM / retention / merchandising

Customer data, lifecycle friction, promotions, conversion and repeat purchase.

How I would turn insight into executive outreach

Commerce leader
Lead with growth and speed

Conversion, ability to launch faster, market expansion, merchandising freedom, and fewer constraints on the revenue team.

CIO / CTO
Lead with simplification

Total platform complexity, developer productivity, integrations, reliability, and the cost of maintaining the current architecture.

CFO
Lead with economic tradeoffs

Total cost of ownership, agency and development spend, payback, operational efficiency, and risk of delaying the change.

Retail / COO
Lead with unified operations

Store efficiency, online and in-store continuity, inventory visibility, and fewer disconnected systems.

Partner-led prospecting

Partners are account intelligence

I would map the agencies, system integrators, and technology partners already touching my priority accounts. They often know where the current stack is expensive, where leadership is frustrated, and when a replatforming conversation is becoming real.

Build mutual account plans

I would form real working relationships with the partners that matter in my territory, exchange account hypotheses, identify where we can create value together, and make partner-sourced or partner-influenced pipeline a repeatable motion.

My first 90 days at Shopify

The first goal would be to compress the learning curve without waiting to prospect. I would learn from the best reps, customers, partners, and internal AI tools while building my territory point of view in parallel.

Days 1 to 30

Absorb

  • Shadow 30+ enterprise customer calls across discovery, technical evaluation, commercial conversations, and close.
  • Identify 3 leading enterprise reps to study closely: one strong prospector, one strong discovery seller, and one strong deal orchestrator.
  • Practice daily with Shopify's proprietary LLMs and internal AI tools: discovery roleplay, objection handling, account research, call debriefs, and competitive positioning.
  • Meet Solutions Engineering, Customer Success, Implementation, Partner teams, and the people who know where enterprise deals most often stall.
  • Mine closed-lost and no-decision accounts to build an initial territory heatmap.
  • Learn the strongest customer stories across B2C, Retail, B2B, international growth, and platform migration.
Days 31 to 60

Create

  • Launch focused outbound around three repeatable plays: closed-lost recycle, platform-change signals, and business expansion signals.
  • Build multi-threaded account maps for the highest-potential accounts, including ground-level operators, technical stakeholders, and executive buyers.
  • Lead my own discovery calls and review them with managers, top reps, and the internal AI tools to tighten messaging quickly.
  • Build working relationships with the agencies, SIs, and technology partners most relevant to the territory.
  • Run joint account planning with partners where they have existing credibility or implementation context.
  • Turn early learning into a territory-specific signal library: what is converting into meetings, and why.
Days 61 to 90

Scale

  • Own full discovery and multi-threaded progression on active opportunities.
  • Establish a weekly pipeline operating rhythm across closed-lost, signals, partner influence, and targeted outbound.
  • Move the best early opportunities from curiosity into quantified business cases with clear decision processes.
  • Build recurring partner and internal account-review cadences around the highest-value enterprise pursuits.
  • Develop a repeatable point of view by vertical and trigger, rather than starting every account from zero.
  • Share what is working with the team so the first 90 days create reusable learning, not just individual pipeline.
